Privacy policy: Nessaid Calculator

Last updated 4 November 2026

Nessaid Calculator is an Android app published by Nessaid Android Studio. It is a calculator with scientific, programmer and statistics modes, a unit converter, finance calculators and a currency converter. This policy explains what it does and does not collect.

What the app collects: nothing

The app has no account, no sign-in and no server of its own. It does not ask for your name, your email address or any other personal detail, and there is nowhere for it to send one. The app has no analytics and no crash reporting of its own. The ad libraries it includes send their own data to the companies behind them, as described under Advertising.

Nothing you calculate leaves your device. No expression you type, no answer, no stored value, no figure you enter into a finance form and no amount you convert is ever transmitted anywhere. The things that do use the network, exchange rates and advertising, are described below, and none of them carries any of it.

What stays on your device

  • Your current line and its history, so the app opens where you left it.
  • Memory and letter registers, and the statistics data list.
  • Your settings: mode, digit count, angle unit, number format, number base and word size, the case, the language, whether a second press of equals repeats the last operation, and which rate sources the currency converter may use.
  • The last exchange rates fetched, with the date the source published them, so the converter works offline and does not fetch again for an hour.
  • Small records the app keeps for itself: when a full-screen ad was last shown, and whether you have bought Remove ads, with when Google Play last confirmed it.

All of it is in the app's own private storage on the phone, and uninstalling the app removes it. Nothing in that list leaves the device, except that if backup is switched on in your phone's settings, Android may keep a copy of your settings in your own Google account, or carry them in a transfer to a new phone. That is Android's backup, and we never receive it. It never includes the exchange rates or the ad, consent or purchase records (Google Play restores a purchase by itself).

Exchange rates

The currency converter downloads a table of rates. The request is a plain fetch of a public file from one of a handful of rate services; it carries no account, no key and no identifier, and it says nothing about you or about what you are converting. The app asks for one file covering every currency and does the arithmetic on your phone, so the service is never told which pair you are interested in, let alone the amount.

It fetches at most once an hour while the converter is open, and when you press the refresh key. As with any internet request, the service can see the IP address it came from. You can choose which of the services the app may use, in the menu under Rate sources.

Advertising

The app shows one banner above the keypad, and an occasional full-screen ad on the way back from the operations guide. Ads come from two ad networks, Google AdMob and Yandex Mobile Ads: for each ad the app asks one, and asks the other only if the first has nothing to show. Some ads are for our own other apps; those are chosen on the phone from a list the app fetches from this website about once a day, and that request carries nothing about you beyond the IP address every internet request carries.

Google's ad SDK sends four kinds of information to Google in order to select and measure ads: your device's advertising ID, your interactions with the ads themselves, diagnostic information about how the SDK performed, and your IP address, from which an approximate location can be estimated.

Yandex's ad SDK sends Yandex your device's advertising ID and the information an ad request carries, such as your IP address, the device model and its language, to select and measure ads. It includes AppMetrica, Yandex's analytics library, which Yandex uses for its ads; the app switches off its reporting of ad events and does not use it for anything of its own. The app also switches off Yandex's location tracking and leaves out AppMetrica's location component.

The app requests no location permission and cannot see where you are; any estimate either company makes comes from the IP address every internet connection carries. What the SDKs do is Google's and Yandex's processing, not ours. We receive no personal data from either and see only aggregate performance figures. This is also what the app declares on its Google Play data safety page, and the two are meant to say the same thing.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, and opt out of ad personalisation, in your phone's settings under Google, then Ads. The calculator works the same either way; the ads simply become less relevant.

Removing the ads

Remove ads, in the menu and in About, is a one-time purchase through Google Play. After it the app shows no ads and does not start either ad SDK at all; nothing else in the app changes. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play: we never see your card or payment details. Google gives us a record of the order (an order number, the product, the price and the date) so that we can handle refunds. The app asks Google Play whether you have bought it, and remembers the answer on the phone without sending it anywhere; when you reinstall or move to a new phone with the same Google account, Google Play restores the purchase.

Consent, for users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form before requesting any ad, and your answer is stored on your device by Google's User Messaging Platform and passed to both ad networks. Choosing not to consent to personalised ads does not restrict any part of the calculator. Where that form was shown, the app's menu has Privacy choices, which opens it again at any time.

Permissions, and why

The app itself requests no permissions. The ones you may see listed come from the ad SDKs and Google Play's billing library, which declare them in their own manifests:

  • Internet and network state: used to load ads and the list of our own apps shown in them, and to download exchange rates. Nothing else in the app uses the network.
  • Advertising ID: used only by the ad SDKs, Google AdMob and Yandex Mobile Ads, as described above.
  • Wake lock and foreground service: declared by Google's ad SDK. The calculator does not run in the background, does not keep your screen awake, and starts no service of its own.
  • Basic phone state: declared by Google's ad SDK. The app does not read it.
  • Install referrer: declared by Yandex's ad SDK, which uses it to tell which store installed the app.
  • Google Play billing: for the Remove ads purchase.

None of these prompts you. They are install-time declarations, not permissions you are asked to grant.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Selling data

We do not sell your data. We have none to sell.
