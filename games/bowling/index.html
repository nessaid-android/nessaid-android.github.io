Nessaid Bowling

Nessaid Bowling is ten pins at the end of a maple lane. Time the swing, hold for the power, and steer the ball home - alone, against bots, or at a table of phones in the same room.

About Nessaid Bowling

Nothing is aimed for you. The ball swings back and forth across the foul line and keeps swinging; wherever it is when you let go is where it starts from. Hold it at the bottom to arm it and its ring fills over two seconds - the fuller the ring, the faster the ball, and letting go early is a softer roll. Changed your mind halfway? Touch the screen with a second finger and the ball is let down, with nothing bowled.

Once it lands the stick turns it, and that is where the hook comes from. Your first push picks the way it drifts and the drift keeps on after you let go; pushing back the other way only slows it, at a quarter the rate, so a hook started too early is a hook you live with. Up and down add or take off a little speed.

Ten frames, scored the way a scoresheet does it: a strike is ten and your next two balls, a spare is ten and your next one. Touch the scores along the top and the whole sheet opens. The button at the far end switches between the bowler's view down the lane and the view from directly above, which is the one to use when a split needs reading.

On one phone it is you, or you against up to three bots you keep and name yourself - a bot's skill is simply how close to its own line it rolls. At a table, one phone hosts over Wi-Fi, a hotspot, Bluetooth or Nearby and the others join; no internet is needed at any point. Bumpers are a setting for the games you set up, and difficulty from 1 to 5 decides how fast the ball swings and how little the stick turns it.

The lane, the ball and the pins are drawn rather than photographed - no image files anywhere in the app - and the physics steps at a fixed rate with no randomness of its own, so the same roll against the same rack knocks the same pins on every phone at the table.

Features

  • A swing you time yourself: where the ball is when you let go is where it rolls from
  • Power on a two-second ring, and a second finger to let the ball down unbowled
  • A hook you steer after the ball lands, and have to live with once it is started
  • Ten frames scored properly, with the full sheet a touch away
  • The bowler's view down the lane, or the view from above for reading a split
  • Up to four players: you, bots you keep and name, or nearby phones
  • Bumpers and five difficulties, for the games you set up
  • A table over Wi-Fi, a hotspot, Bluetooth or Nearby, with no internet at all
  • The same roll knocks the same pins on every phone, by construction
  • Thirty-three languages
Nessaid Bowling is in development and is not on Google Play yet.
