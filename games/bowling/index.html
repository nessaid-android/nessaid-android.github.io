Nessaid Bowling

Nessaid Bowling is ten pins at the end of a maple lane. One swipe up the lane is the whole throw - alone, against bots, or at a table of phones in the same room.

About Nessaid Bowling

Nothing is aimed for you. The ball starts on the board your finger first touches, as the lane is drawn - right out to the edge board, with part of the ball over the gutter; start beside the lane and it is in the gutter. Straight is along the boards as they are drawn where your swipe began, so a swipe that traces the boards rolls down them, and one straight up the screen from the side drifts out. How far and how fast you swipe is the power. The direction is yours, gutter and all - swipe across the lane and that is where the ball goes. Changed your mind halfway? Stop your finger and lift it, or touch with a second finger, and nothing is bowled. How to swipe, in the menu, bowls fourteen swipes on the lane as you see it, one after another, and says what each shows.

Bend the end of the swipe and the ball hooks that way once it has skidded and gripped the lane. The hook is held to what a real ball can do however hard the swipe is bent, so a ball aimed out and hooked back comes back the way a real hook does - and a bend that is only your thumb's own arc is no hook at all.

Ten frames, scored the way a scoresheet does it: a strike is ten and your next two balls, a spare is ten and your next one. Touch the scores along the top and the whole sheet opens. The button at the far end of the bar switches between the bowler's view down the lane and the view from directly above, which is the one to use when a split needs reading. Beside it is the chalk: with it on, each throw leaves your swipe and the ball's path marked until the view is back behind the foul line - or, if you choose, with the pins as they fell until you touch the arrow to go on.

Every game bowled to its end is recorded on your phone - alone, against bots, or at a table, whether you hosted it or joined it. Its last screen has Replay and Save. A replay is the whole game as your phone showed it: every bowler's balls in order and the scores filling in, with pause, next ball and a speed of its own. Saved games, on the first screen, keeps up to fifty, each named by its date, its bowlers and the winning score; touch one to watch it again, or its cross to delete it. Nothing of it leaves the phone.

The game itself can be shown at any of six speeds, from half speed to twice as fast - the roll, the pins and the waits between balls - and not one throw changes: the same swipe knocks the same pins at every speed. Alone, against bots or in practice it is a button on the bar, to change at any time.

On one phone it is you, or you against bots you keep and name yourself - a bot's skill is simply how close to its own line it rolls. At a table, one phone hosts over Wi-Fi, a hotspot, Bluetooth or Nearby and the others join, up to six bowlers; no internet is needed at any point. Over Bluetooth a host you have joined can be saved: next time it is called first, and ticked it is the only phone called, so joining again takes seconds instead of a search. Bumpers, difficulty from 1 to 5 - the harder, the less the ball hooks - the turn factor, take-backs (a second after each swipe to cancel it) and the speed are the host's to set, and everybody at the table sees them and agrees before the first ball.

Practice is you alone on a lane you can change: its turn factor, from no hook at all to twice the hook, and its length, from 10 to 25 metres. A score against anybody else is always a score on the regulation lane.

There are eight balls to choose from, and the others at a table see the one you roll. The lane, the balls and the pins are drawn rather than photographed - no image files anywhere in the app - and the physics steps at a fixed rate with no randomness of its own, so the same roll against the same rack knocks the same pins on every phone at the table.

Features

  • One swipe is the throw: where it starts, where it is aimed, how hard, and how much it hooks
  • The ball starts on the board under your finger, edge board included, and straight is along the boards
  • A direction that is yours, gutter and all, and a hook held to what a real ball can do
  • Every finished game recorded: watch it again, and keep up to fifty in Saved games
  • Six speeds, from half to twice as fast, with every throw the same
  • Chalk: your swipe and the ball's path marked on the lane after each throw
  • Practice on a lane you set: its turn factor, and its length from 10 to 25 metres
  • Ten frames scored properly, with the full sheet a touch away
  • The bowler's view down the lane, or the view from above for reading a split
  • Up to six bowlers: you, bots you keep and name, or nearby phones
  • Bumpers, five difficulties, the turn factor, take-backs and the speed, agreed by the table before the first ball
  • Eight balls to choose from, seen by everybody at the table
  • A table over Wi-Fi, a hotspot, Bluetooth or Nearby, with no internet at all
  • Saved phones over Bluetooth: the host you joined is called first next time
  • The same roll knocks the same pins on every phone, by construction
  • Thirty-three languages, and text that follows your phone's font size
