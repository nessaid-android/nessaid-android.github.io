Privacy policy: Nessaid Bowling

Last updated 4 November 2026

Nessaid Bowling is a ten-pin bowling game for Android, published by Nessaid Android Studio. You bowl with one swipe up the lane, alone, against bots, or with other phones in the same room. This policy explains what it does and does not collect.

What the app collects: nothing

The app has no account, no sign-in and no server of its own. Your games, your scores, the name you play under and your settings are stored on your device only, and nobody, the developer included, can see them. The app has no analytics, no crash reporting and no usage tracking of its own.

What leaves your device over the internet is advertising data, collected by the ad networks, Google AdMob and Yandex Mobile Ads, so that they can serve the ads in the app, and a once-a-day request to this website for the list of our own apps the ads may show. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland you are asked before any ad is requested.

What stays on your device

  • The name you play under, and the bots you have named, to put them on the scoresheet.
  • Your settings: the language, sound, bumpers, the difficulty, the turn factor, the speed the game is shown at, your ball, the view, and whether your throws are chalked.
  • Games you have played: the last game bowled to its end, and any you saved, so that you can watch them again. Each holds the names of everybody who bowled in it, their throws and their scores. Delete a saved game from Saved games.
  • Phones you chose to save when you joined a game over Bluetooth: each one's name and Bluetooth address, so that it is called first the next time. Forget any of them from the Phones list on Join.
  • A key made on your phone the first time you play with others, so that the phones in a game can tell each other apart. It never leaves the phones you play with.
  • Small records the app keeps for itself: when a full-screen ad was last shown, and whether you have bought Remove ads, with when Google Play last confirmed it.

All of it is in the app's own private storage on the phone, and uninstalling the app removes it.

What goes to the other phones in a game

When you host or join a game with other phones, your phone talks directly to those phones, in the same room: over Wi-Fi, a hotspot, Bluetooth, or Google's Nearby connection between phones. It does not go through the internet. What goes between them is the game, and only to the phones in it:

  • The name you play under. Your phone's model name is added to it for the game, so that two phones in a room can be told apart.
  • Your throws, the ball you chose, and the scores.

Nothing is sent to the developer or to any server. A game ends when its host closes it, and nothing of it remains except on the phones that were in it.

Advertising

The app shows a banner ad on its screens, and occasionally a full-screen ad after a game has finished. Ads come from two ad networks, Google AdMob and Yandex Mobile Ads: for each ad the app asks one, and asks the other only if the first has nothing to show. Advertising is the only part of the app that uses the internet.

To select and measure ads, Google's ad SDK collects and processes your device's advertising ID, your interactions with the ads themselves, device and app information such as the model, the Android version and the app's version, and your IP address, from which an approximate location can be estimated.

Yandex's ad SDK sends Yandex your device's advertising ID and the information an ad request carries, such as your IP address, the device model and its language, to select and measure ads. It includes AppMetrica, Yandex's analytics library, which Yandex uses for its ads; the app switches off its reporting of ad events and does not use it for anything of its own. The app also switches off Yandex's location tracking and leaves out AppMetrica's location component, so Yandex is never given a position by the app.

The app itself never reads your location; any estimate either company makes comes from the IP address every internet connection carries. What the SDKs do is Google's and Yandex's processing, not ours. We receive no personal data from either and see only aggregate performance figures. This is also what the app declares on its Google Play data safety page, and the two are meant to say the same thing.

Some ads are for other Nessaid Android apps. Those are drawn by the app itself from pictures already inside it, and chosen on the phone from a list the app fetches from this website about once a day; that request carries nothing about you beyond the IP address every internet request carries. Touching one opens that app's page in Google Play.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, and opt out of ad personalisation, in your phone's settings under Google, then Ads. The game works the same either way; the ads simply become less relevant.

Removing the ads

Remove ads is a one-time purchase through Google Play. After it the app shows no ads and does not start either ad SDK at all; nothing else in the game changes. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play: we never see your card or payment details. Google gives us a record of the order (an order number, the product, the price and the date) so that we can handle refunds. The app asks Google Play whether you have bought it, and remembers the answer on the phone without sending it anywhere; when you reinstall or move to a new phone with the same Google account, Google Play restores the purchase.

Consent, for users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form before requesting any ad, and your answer is stored on your device by Google's User Messaging Platform and passed to both ad networks. Choosing not to consent to personalised ads does not restrict any part of the game. Where that form was shown, Settings ends with Privacy choices, which opens it again at any time.

Permissions, and why

A game on one phone, alone or against bots, asks for nothing. The permissions below are asked for only when you host or join a game with other phones, and only the ones the way you chose needs.

  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth, and Wi-Fi devices): finding the other phones in the room and connecting to them. If you refuse, you cannot play with other phones that way; everything else works.
  • Location, approximate and precise, on Android 12L and older only: on those versions Android does not allow a search for nearby phones over Bluetooth, or over Google's Nearby connection, without it. A phone that hosts a game over Bluetooth is not asked. The app never reads your location and never sends it anywhere. If you refuse, those phones cannot find a game that way.
  • Notifications: the notice shown while a game with other phones is running, which is also the way back to it. If you refuse, the game still runs and the notice is hidden.

The rest are install-time declarations that never prompt you:

  • Run a foreground service (connected device), and keep the phone awake for it: keeping your place in a game with other phones while the app is not in front, so that the others are not left waiting.
  • Wi-Fi state and multicast: finding a game on the same Wi-Fi network or hotspot.
  • Internet, network state and the advertising ID: used only for advertising, by the ad SDKs, Google AdMob and Yandex Mobile Ads, and for the list of our own apps shown in ads, as described above.
  • Install referrer: declared by Yandex's ad SDK, which uses it to tell which store installed the app.
  • Google Play billing: for the Remove ads purchase.

The code that joins a game can be scanned with the camera. That uses Google's own scanner, which is part of Google Play services: the app has no camera permission and never sees the picture. The game does not use your microphone, contacts, files or phone.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Selling data

We do not sell your data. We have none to sell.

Your data rights

Because everything except advertising data stays on your device, there is no account to delete and nothing for us to hand over or erase. Uninstalling the app removes all of it. For the advertising data the controllers are Google and Yandex, and their privacy policies, linked above, cover access and deletion.
