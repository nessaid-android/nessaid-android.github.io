Baraja Española: Spanish Deck

Baraja Española: Spanish Deck is thirteen card games of the Spanish pack in one app, each with its rules as they are played in Spain and around it, for the phones around one table: against bots, or with friends on their own phones, with no internet at all.

The thirteen games

  • Brisca: Three cards, a trump turned up, and the aces and threes to take. Two or four players.
  • Tute: Follow suit, sing the twenty and the forty, and take the last trick. Four in pairs, or two.
  • Guiñote: Aragón's cousin of Tute: six cards and a stock, the twenty and the forty, and the first pair to 101.
  • Escoba: Take what makes fifteen with a card from your hand, and sweep the table for an escoba. Two players, or four in pairs.
  • Cinquillo: Build the four suits out from their fives, a card at a time; the first out wins. Two to four players.
  • Chinchón: The Spanish rummy: draw, throw, meld sets and runs, and close with little left loose. Two to four players.
  • Mus: Two pairs, four cards, four lances: grande, chica, pares and juego. Envido, quiero, órdago.
  • Sueca: Follow suit, take the aces and the sevens, and pass sixty. Four in pairs.
  • Butifarra: Choose trumps or say butifarra, double with contro, and take the nines. Four in pairs.
  • Pocha: Say how many tricks you will take, and take exactly that. Three to five, each alone.
  • Ronda: Take your card's rank and the run up from it; score rondas, tringas, darbas and missas. Two, or four in pairs.
  • Siete y medio: Get as near seven and a half as you dare, without going over, and beat the bank. Two to six.
  • Julepe: Play the hand or pass; every trick takes a fifth of the pot, and fewer than two is a julepe. Three to six.

The rules of every game

Made for a real table

  • No internet, no mobile data. One phone hosts and the others join over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, a phone's hotspot or Google Nearby.
  • Everyone on their own phone. Nobody passes a handset round, and nobody sees a hand that is not theirs.
  • Short of players? Bots take the empty seats, each with a setting for how closely it follows the play.
  • The whole game in History, hand by hand and card by card, and an export to share it.
  • A dropped link is not the end of the hand. A phone that loses the connection can take its seat again.
  • Fourteen languages: English, Spanish, Catalan, Basque, Galician, Italian, Portuguese, Brazilian Portuguese, French, Arabic, Filipino, Russian, Ukrainian and Polish.
  • No sign-in and no real money. The chips in Siete y medio and Julepe are counters for one game and are worth nothing.
